Programs

REACH runs three linked programs. The fellowship trains researchers and produces the analysis, the studio turns that analysis into public evidence, and the impact lab tests the small share of it that a partner can act on.

This page describes the methods and areas in which REACH works. Individual project titles are not listed here while work is underway. Completed outputs appear on the publications page after public release.

Research Fellowship

A selective sixteen-week research program. Fellows join a small team on a defined question, follow a written protocol, and carry the work through to a documented output. Four weeks of common methods training are embedded across the program rather than front-loaded.

The first cohort is designed around these parameters:

Research Fellowship, first cohort design
Fellows8 to 10
Projects3
Fellows per project2 to 4
AdvisersOne faculty or senior adviser per project
Weekly workload4 to 6 hours
Common trainingFour weeks, embedded across the program
Public outputA methods page and an entry in the completed-output registry

Open Evidence Studio

The studio is not a separate fellowship. It is the production function that converts finished research into things other people can actually use:

  • Reproducible analysis code and documented data
  • Evidence briefs and technical reports
  • Public figures and methods explainers
  • Data notes and partner-ready summaries

Everything the studio releases is governed by our open-science standards.

Impact Lab

The Impact Lab selects only findings a legitimate partner can use. A pilot does not start until every one of these is defined:

  • One problem
  • One partner
  • One setting
  • One decision-maker
  • One intervention
  • One implementation period
  • One measurable adoption outcome

A short pilot cannot show that population health improved, and we do not claim it did. We measure adoption, reach, usability, fidelity, continuation, and operational or policy change.

Global health equity

A comparative strand examining how different health systems produce different equity outcomes, including diaspora health research. It uses the same public-data, no-human-subjects approach as the rest of our portfolio, and it operates under UT review where international collaboration is involved.
