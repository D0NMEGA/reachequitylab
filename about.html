About Us

REACH Equity Lab is a student-led research initiative at the University of Texas at Austin, focused on health disparities across racial, wage, and healthcare access gaps. We train emerging researchers, produce reproducible evidence from open public data, and help partners translate that evidence into practice.

We are an undergraduate organization, not an institutional research center. What we can offer is careful work, done in the open, that other people can check and reuse.

Mission

Advance equity by producing clear, data-informed research on the disparities that shape health outcomes, and by making that research usable to the people positioned to act on it.

Theory of change

REACH converts open public data into rigorous research, understandable public evidence, and small real-world interventions. The three steps are deliberately linked: research that never becomes legible to a practitioner does not change anything, and a pilot that is not grounded in evidence is just activity.

  1. Open data in

    Public aggregate datasets, government statistical tables, and openly licensed sources that can be analyzed without collecting identifiable information.

  2. Rigorous analysis

    A written protocol and analysis plan before the work starts, and independent methods review before anything is claimed.

  3. Public evidence

    Code, documentation, figures, and plain-language briefs released together so the finding can be verified and reused.

  4. Practical use

    Where a partner can act, a scoped pilot tests whether the evidence is usable in one real setting.

What REACH means

Research in Epidemiology, Access, and Community Health.

  • R: Research — We ground every initiative in careful inquiry and evidence-driven analysis, working from public and openly licensed datasets.
  • E: Epidemiology — We apply epidemiologic methods to quantify disparities across populations and identify modifiable risk factors.
  • A: Access — We examine barriers to care, affordability, and the pathways people use to seek help.
  • C: Community — We align findings with lived experience and local context, and prioritize outputs communities can act on directly.
  • H: Health — Every project is oriented toward practical recommendations for clinicians, policymakers, and advocates.

How we govern the work

These are the standards REACH holds itself to. They exist so that no single person controls research decisions, money, authorship, code, partnerships, and media at the same time.

  • Separated authority. Research questions and methods, spending, public claims, and media are each owned by a different director, with a faculty adviser consulted on strategy.
  • Evidence-based authorship. Authorship follows ICMJE criteria and documented contribution, not seniority or title.
  • Independent review. Analytical code is reviewed by someone who did not write it, and a reproducibility check runs before any public release.
  • No human subjects in year one. Our current portfolio avoids participant recruitment and identifiable data entirely.
  • Nothing published early. Work in progress, submitted manuscripts, and prospective collaborations do not appear on this site. See our publication rule.
